# Unslop

Rewrite text so it reads like a person wrote it. The meaning and facts as well as voice should remain as they are. Only rewrite the text to remove slop tainted elements.

## Steps

1. Work out what the text is actually saying. You must not add or drop claims.
2. Find the patterns listed below and cut or rewrite them.
3. Check each sentence. If it would fit equally well in an essay on any other subject, give it a specific detail or delete it.
4. Hand back the revised text. Only describe your changes if the user would want to know.

## What to cut

**Padding**
- Introductions and endings that echo the request or recap what was just said, such as "In conclusion", "It's important to note that", and "Let's dive in".
- Qualifiers piled onto every claim, such as "can potentially" and "in many cases".
- Closing offers such as "I hope this helps" and "Feel free to ask".

**Stock words**
- delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, testament, navigate, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, robust, holistic, pivotal, crucial, vibrant, myriad, foster, underscore, ever-evolving, game-changer.
- Swap each for an ordinary word or a concrete fact.

**Stock structure**
- Contrasts like "not just X, but Y" and "it's not about X, it's about Y".
- Groups of three used out of habit. List as many items as there really are.
- Questions the text answers straight away.
- Paragraphs that are all the same size and each finish on a tidy lesson.
- Headings, bullets, and bold applied to text that works fine as plain paragraphs.
- Recaps of each section in a short piece.
- Two clauses stuck together with a semicolon or similar. Write two sentences or add a conjunction.
- Terse command pairs such as "Do X. Prefer X over Y." or "Use X, not Y." Say how to apply the rule and why, so the reader can handle cases the pair doesn't cover.

**Tone**
- Enthusiasm, praise, or sympathy the content doesn't call for.
- Sales language in neutral writing.
- Em dashes. Remove every one and use a comma, period, colon, or parentheses.
- Emoji, unless the surrounding context already uses them.

## Guidelines

- Choose specific nouns and verbs over vague ones, and include the actual number, name, or example.
- Keep sentences short and direct, with natural variety in length.
- Leave in a hedge when the uncertainty is real.
- Never plant errors, new claims, or typos.
- Keep the original register. Technical writing should stay technical.
- If the text is already plain, say so and change very little.
